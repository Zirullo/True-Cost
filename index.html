True Cost: what if your car told you exactly what each km costs?

Drivers think in money, not in litres. Yet no cockpit shows the real cost of mobility in real time. This project proves it is already possible using Stellantis Connected Services and live price data.

⛽ Live consumption

Cost per km = consumption × pump price. Consumption follows speed on a real fuel-map curve — hold the ↑ Accelerate / ↓ Brake pedals (or the arrow keys) and watch the € / km move.

● Live prices

Local Price is the price on the totem of the next station ahead, with the distance to it counting down — watch for it on the right of the road. Stations scatter about 15 cents either side of the live local average (MIMIT open data, stations within 50 km), with the motorway ones further above and the whitelabels below — and the same totem is the row you see in the pump map, under along this road. Last Refuel Price is what the fuel in the tank actually cost — and the trip cost is computed on that.

● Trip & tank

Distance, litres burned and total euro accumulate live; the fuel level bar drains with them. Press RESET — on the cluster or on the deck — to start a new trip from zero. Press ENGINE on the deck (or “E”) to switch the engine off: the car coasts to a stop, and a running engine standing still burns about 0.7 L/h that buys no distance at all — the cost of going nowhere.

▣ Centre display

The screen on the dashboard runs the app itself, and it is touchable: Cost history charts what every kilometre has cost you — one bar per 200 m of the road you are driving — over your previous trips: tap any of them to open it, with its own cost profile, litres, average speed and the price paid at the pump. Pump map — the view the demo opens on — ranks the stations around the car by price, cheapest in green with the halo, dearest in red: choose how far to look — 5, 10 or 20 km, or along this road, which is not a radius but the totems you are about to drive past, each one at the price its sign will show through the windshield, or on my route, the same corridor for as long as no destination is set — then tap a station and NAVIGATE to be driven there by a simulated navigator, arrow by arrow. Price history puts two years of the Italian pump price behind our own refuels: pick the window and how finely to sample it, and tap any stop to see what we paid against the market that day. Report is the view for whoever pays rather than drives: pick a week, a month or single trips, keep only the business ones or only the private ones, and the log becomes an expense claim — spend, kilometres, litres, €/km, the business / private split and the recoverable VAT — ready for email, CSV, a fleet portal, an expense tool or a ride‑hailing ledger. The delivery is simulated: nothing leaves this page. Trips owns the question the other four all depend on: where does one trip end? Choose what closes it — the ignition, smart (the engine has to stay off for two, five, fifteen or thirty minutes, so a fuel stop stays inside the trip and going home does not), the GPS destination (the trip ends where you said it would: pick a station in Pumps, press NAVIGATE, and it closes on arrival — with no destination set the pane says so instead of guessing), or nothing but your own hand — then switch the engine off and watch the countdown ring run down. Keep open holds the trip through a stop, split here cuts it in two, and closing one brings up the card that files it: business, private or commute, merged back into the previous trip, or thrown away. What you save goes straight to the top of Costs and into the Report, priced on the fuel it actually burned.
